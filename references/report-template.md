# Report Structure: Progressive Market Opportunity Research

Use this as a **flexible outline**, not a rigid list. Merge sections when the question is narrow; add deep dives when asked. Start with a short thesis and the exact commercial decision being evaluated.

## 0. Executive snapshot
- What question are we answering?
- 2–3 supported findings and the critical uncertainty.
- One provisional recommendation, conditional on evidence.

## 1. Frame the market from first principles
- What job is the buyer trying to get done, triggered by what event?
- Who is the user, champion, payer and approver? Is the problem frequent or expensive?
- Technical distinction that prevents choosing the wrong solution (only when material).

## 2. Demand: proof versus enthusiasm
- 2–4 dated evidence points, with region, sample, source links and limitations.
- Strongest payment/deployment signals.
- Counter-signals and what data is missing.

## 3. Understand the landscape
Table idea:

| Named player (link) | Layer / buyer | What they actually sell | Verified pricing / model | Customers / proof | What they leave unresolved |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Explain layers: direct product, complement, infrastructure, implementation service, do-it-yourself/manual substitutes. If the user asks about founder journeys, provide sourced chronology rather than only the market map.

## 4. Case studies: what exactly happened?
For 2–4 meaningful cases, where evidence exists:

- Company/founder, timeframe, initial problem and first wedge.
- Real deployment or customer, actual solution and integration path.
- Dated outcome, quantified claim and whose claim it is.
- What can transfer to this user, what cannot, and the original source link.

## 5. Strategic options and tradeoffs

| Option | Ideal customer & value | Time-to-test | Distribution & sales | Delivery & maintenance | Risks | Evidence to choose it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Compare options on *different strategic choices*, not cosmetic variants. Include buy/integrate, service-first, product-first or partnership only where relevant.

## 6. Commercial mechanics
- What is the offer and measurable unit of value?
- Who pays? For what? Why recurring vs one-time?
- Public market price anchors versus untested proposed price.
- Simple, transparent ROI/unit economics example with explicit assumptions.
- Delivery costs: acquisition, integration, model/infrastructure, data, QA, security and support.

## 7. Practical recommendation
- Leading hypothesis and why, given the user's resources and timeframe.
- 1–2 alternatives rejected/deferred and what would change the choice.
- Explicit unverified claims.

## 8. Minimum experiment (7–30 days or user timeframe)
- Which buyer profile to speak with; where to find them.
- Evidence to collect from real past incidents.
- Bounded demo or paid discovery deliverable; minimum dataset and permissions.
- Success/failure criteria: measurable behaviour, actual commitment and willingness to pay.
- Kill conditions and decision at the end.

## 9. Sources and research limits
- Clickable primary sources with dates, region and evidence type.
- If missing, say so and identify next validation steps.

### Optional modules
- Deep founder/funding timeline, regulatory analysis, technology build-vs-buy, competitor feature matrix, outbound prospect list, positioning message, pricing tests, market-size model. Use only if requested or materially required.

### Style requirements
- Answer in Chinese if the user asks in Chinese; use natural bilingual terms when helpful.
- Define specialist terms at first appearance.
- Explain *why each section matters for the decision*, not only what exists.
- Tables for comparison, paragraphs for causal explanation, no forced scoring.
- Do not ask the user to read a giant report to learn the recommendation; lead with the thesis and surface limitations.
