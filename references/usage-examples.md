# Examples: Same Skill, Different Markets

These are illustrations of how the research workflow transfers. They are not market findings and contain no current statistics.

## Example 1: Personal AI → SME knowledge assistant
User: "I have a private AI that can recall previous conversations. Could I sell something like this to smaller companies?"

Adaptation:
- Separate keeping data, retrieving documents, workflow automation and training model weights.
- Compare internal support assistant, SaaS embedded copilot, integration service and memory SDK.
- Map real players, documented customer deployments and system integrators.
- Identify new enterprise requirements: permission-aware retrieval, tenants, source traceability, data residency and monitoring.
- Validate one repeated support question workflow and a paid pilot.

## Example 2: Motion design tooling
User: "I'm a designer. Could I build an indie product that owns one small corner of motion design?"

Adaptation:
- Start from specific creator jobs and current manual workflow, not a generic 'motion platform'.
- Map editors, design systems, export/code handoff products, specialists and free alternatives.
- Find public founder/customer stories and pricing; distinguish creator enthusiasm from payment.
- Compare paid plugin, standalone tool, hosted service and developer SDK.
- Propose a tiny paid workflow improvement with measurable time saved.

## Example 3: Manufacturing technical knowledge
User: "Could an AI help field engineers answer questions from service manuals and old tickets?"

Adaptation:
- Focus on fault triage or document location as a single job, not autonomous machine control.
- Include industrial incumbents, niche integrators, internal manual process and data access blockers.
- Require traceable manuals, machine/version context and escalation rules.
- Separate pilot answer quality from plant-safety and enterprise production commitments.

## Example 4: Founder journey request
User: "How did [small SaaS] make its first $10k? Find founder, timeline, first distribution and primary evidence."

Adaptation:
- Lead with verifiable founder and chronological actions.
- Link interviews, archived posts, pricing history, launch notes, customer examples.
- Do not infer first revenue from site traffic or public popularity.
- Compare repeatable tactics, constraints and differences from the user's position.
