# Reusable Intake: Market Opportunity Research

Fill **only what you know**. One sentence describing the idea is enough to start; missing details can become assumptions or open questions. Do not force a questionnaire onto the user.

- **Idea / question**: What might we build, sell, or explore?
- **What I currently have** (optional): prototype, relevant experience, existing users, distribution, data, assets.
- **Potential buyer / industry / geography** (optional): who might pay; what region matters.
- **Personal constraints** (optional): solo builder or team, time, capital, data access, regulatory constraints.
- **Success definition** (optional): first customer, $10k revenue, recurring SaaS, open-source adoption, consultancy, learning.
- **Desired depth** (optional): 15-minute scan, evidence-rich study, founder journey, competitor/price deep dive.
- **Decision to make next** (optional): pursue/pass, target buyer, pricing, service vs software, build vs buy.

## One-line prompts

**New concept**
> Use Market Opportunity Research to evaluate [idea]. Start from actual buyer jobs and current alternatives. Show market evidence, key players, commercial routes, tradeoffs and a concrete first paid validation plan. Use current primary sources and label what isn't verified.

**Competitor and founder research**
> Research [market]. Who are the relevant products and founders, when did they begin, how did they acquire their first customers, what do they charge, and which results can be verified? Give original links, limitations and implications for a small builder.

**Business model choice**
> For [idea], compare product-first, service-first and partnership options through customer, distribution, workload, pricing, risk and how quickly I can test willingness to pay. Recommend a bounded experiment, not a generic strategy.
