# Evidence Standards and Research Checklist

This is a research operating guide, not a static source of current facts. The assistant must collect current evidence when a new question is asked.

## Evidence hierarchy (adapt to question)

| Signal | Example | What it supports | What it does NOT prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verified purchase or contract | Public procurement award, invoice, contract disclosure | Someone paid for something | A repeatable, profitable market |
| Documented customer deployment | Named case study with workflow and date | Someone put a solution into use | Vendor claimed ROI is independently causal |
| Public pricing | Official vendor page with date and units | An available price anchor | Buyers commonly pay this price or retain |
| Independent user experience | Detailed case/report from actual users | Specific job, friction, workarounds | Representative market prevalence |
| Representative survey | Transparent sample and period | Attitudes or behaviours for that sampled group | Global willingness to pay |
| Vendor announcement | Vendor self-description / launch post | Product positioning, claimed feature | Independent effectiveness |
| Analyst inference | Comparison of feature/workflow gaps | An investigation hypothesis | Proven demand |

## Source recording fields

For each important claim, capture:

- Claim in your own words.
- Source name, original URL, publication or observed date, region and sample if applicable.
- Classification: observed / vendor claim / estimate / assumption.
- Metric, unit and denominator (per seat, per response, annual subscription, per company, etc.).
- Confidence or caveat and an alternative interpretation.
- Date checked.

## Market-map research checklist

Look separately for:

1. Direct vendors: features, customer category, distribution, pricing model.
2. Enablers: infra/SDKs, hosting, open-source, agency partnerships.
3. Implementation services: real providers, published offerings, verifiable testimonials and project budget *requests*, if any.
4. Non-software substitutes: staff, spreadsheets, documented processes, generic LLMs, in-house builds.
5. 2–4 customer or founder journeys when relevant: year/trigger, initial product, who first used it, how it distributed, pivot/pricing, credible milestones, current status. Mark missing chronology rather than manufacture it.
6. Objections or failures: community dissatisfaction, security/privacy requirements, switching and procurement barriers.

## Pricing and unit economics

Always separate: buyer price; provider cost; customer's value; realised savings; hypothetical capacity release. One-time implementation revenue differs from recurring software revenue. A high price anchor does not imply the user's new offering can charge the same. A service-directory budget filter does not imply a transaction happened.

When estimating:

`potential benefit = volume × eligible share × actual success share × minutes or dollars per successful case`

Show each assumed variable and which require a customer measurement. Include implementation/support/hosting costs, not only AI token fees.

## Bias counterchecks

- Did we overfit the answer to the user's own prototype?
- Did we find customers purchasing a competitor rather than only suppliers claiming a market?
- Could an existing low-cost product already satisfy the use case?
- Is the actual buyer different from the day-to-day user?
- Can anyone grant access to the relevant data?
- Does a high-security environment make a solo implementation impractical?
- Did we compare the same geographical/time frame and price unit?
- Does the conclusion remain valid if one vendor's case study is marketing exaggeration?

## If sources are thin

Provide a specific evidence gap and what would close it. Keep a shortlist of target organisations, interview questions and procurement proof needed. Do not pad with unsourced TAM or invented metrics.
